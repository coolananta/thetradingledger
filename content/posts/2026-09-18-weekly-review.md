## Wednesday: the day I was too unfocused to even journal

I called it a day right around the open, telling myself I was protecting the account by staying off the laptop while mentally unavailable. What I actually did was miss two of the best setups of the entire week. I was frustrated enough about it the next day that I skipped journaling it entirely — so this is the first time either of these trades has been written down.

### RETO: a real $20.85 high, confirmed independently

<span class="pill pill-china">RETO · China</span> <span class="pill pill-slate">Basic Materials</span>

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Company | ReTo Eco-Solutions — Beijing-based ecological/environmental equipment manufacturer |
| Public Float | ~36M shares (larger than I usually trade) |
| Prior Corporate Action | 4-for-1 reverse split, May 2026, for Nasdaq compliance |
| Catalyst | **None identified** — independently confirmed by multiple real-time market reports the same day |
| Real Confirmed Price Action | $0.36 (prior close) → **$20.85 intraday high** → closed back down at $1.84, same session |

I ignored RETO in the pre-market specifically because the price action was choppy — a reasonable filter in general, but the real move didn't start until after the open anyway, so the pre-market chop was never the part that mattered.

{{< callout type="important" title="The setup, exactly as it happened" >}}
The initial impulse leg ran from about **$0.50 to $8** on September 15th — nearly a $7.50 leg on its own. Price then consolidated until it hit the **9-EMA on the 4-hourly chart** on the 16th. This is one of my favorite patterns by name: a 9-EMA-on-the-4-hour touch, followed by a lower-timeframe pattern, ideally on the break of a multi-month high. RETO delivered all three — it bounced off the 4-hour 9-EMA, broke its **6-month high at $6.22**, double-bottomed on that exact level on the 1-minute chart around 10:08 AM, curled back up, and ran to a real, independently-confirmed high of **$20.85** by 11:17 AM. Price stayed fully contained within the **21-EMA on the 1-minute chart** the entire way up — meaning there was never a technical reason to exit early, all the way from $6 to $20.
{{< /callout >}}

That's roughly **230% in a little over an hour**, on a setup that matched my favorite pattern exactly, on a stock I dismissed for a reason (float size, pre-market chop) that turned out not to matter once the real session started.

| Metric | Value |

The Helomics deal is a genuinely real, structurally clean transaction — no assumed debt, real cash and lab assets received in return for mostly paper. It's also sitting on top of a company burning far more than it earns, which is exactly the profile this journal keeps encountering on its biggest movers: the catalyst is real, the reaction is real, and the balance sheet underneath it is fragile regardless.

{{< callout type="note" title="The setup I had on my own watchlist" >}}
MEDS had been riding the **9-EMA on the hourly chart** for a while. It then formed a **3 hour 15 minute consolidation** directly beneath the 100-day moving average, bouncing off the **9-EMA on the 15-minute chart** multiple times — textbook accumulation. At 9:45 AM, price broke above the 100 DMA and ran from **$4.50 to $12.26**, climaxing around 11 AM. The 1-minute timeframe offered real pyramiding room on the way up.

## Monday: ELMT and FTFT

**[Full breakdown →](/posts/2026-09-14-elmt-ftft/)**

ELMT cost $78.20 across two round trades — a trade the post itself concluded had no real pattern supporting it. While that was happening, FTFT was offering multiple bounces off the 9-EMA on the 15-minute chart after emerging from a double tap of the 9-EMA on the 4-hourly chart — an estimated $400 in 45 minutes on just 100 shares. A second, separate FTFT setup showed up the very next day at $5, right off the same 4-hourly 9-EMA, worth another $400. Combined, that's roughly **$800** left on the table around a single stock, on a day where the actual trade taken had no technical basis at all.

## Tuesday: VEEA

**[Full breakdown →](/posts/2026-09-15-veea/)**

VEEA's impulse leg ran from $1.51 to $4.29 on rising volume, then consolidated for three candles in a tight range on lowered volume, finding support at the 9-EMA on the hourly chart — about as strong a buy signal as this journal tracks. A ride from roughly $3.50 to $7 on 100 shares would have been worth **at least $350**, with a second entry opportunity available for pyramiding on top of that. Zero trades were taken. VEEA was on the watchlist before it moved, which — as the original post on this session said — makes the miss sting more than the dollar figure alone does.

## Thursday: KXIN, AEMD, DAIC

**[Full breakdown →](/posts/2026-09-17-kxin-aemd-daic/)**

Net loss of $4.00. KXIN had nothing going for it beyond a single 9-EMA double tap on the 30-minute chart. AEMD had already given back more than half its gains by the time it was traded, with no real reason to expect another swing — and it predictably sold off from the $10 double top instead. DAIC double-tapped the 9-EMA on the hourly chart at the $4 confluence level and ran from $4 at 9:25 AM to about $7 by 9:50 AM — a real ~$300 opportunity on some risk. Looking back with a clearer head: I wouldn't actually call this an A+ setup. DAIC had already made a big move a month earlier, and the odds of a second big move that close together, without first breaking the prior move's high, are genuinely slim. That's a real refinement worth keeping — not every correctly-identified miss was actually a high-probability trade in hindsight, and DAIC is the clearest example of that distinction this week.

## Friday: IMCC

**[Full breakdown →](/posts/2026-09-18-imcc/)**

The day looked hopeless — most stocks were selling off. IMCC's initial leg ran from $2 to $5, sold off hard, then bounced off the 9-EMA on the hourly chart, built the volume signature of rising price and volume with shrinking retracement volume, broke the pre-market high at $5, retraced to it, curled back up on the 5-second chart, and eventually pushed past the 100 DMA toward $8.50. I was in and out in **19 seconds** for $18. Left alone, that trade was worth an estimated **$350**. The day ended at -$10.

## What actually connects all five days

Nearly every setup this week — FTFT, VEEA, MEDS, RETO, and the Trade 1 version of IMCC — shared the same underlying skeleton: a **higher-timeframe 9-EMA (hourly or 4-hourly)**, confirmed by a **lower-timeframe pattern**, often at a **round-number or multi-month/multi-week high**. That's not a coincidence five separate times in one week. It's the actual edge, restated in the same language every single time it showed up.

The other thread is harder to quantify but matters more: **Wednesday wasn't a rest day, it was the single most expensive day of the week**, and it never got that price tag until right now. Walking away when genuinely unfit to trade is a real skill — this journal has credited itself for exactly that before. But this week is proof that the same decision can be exactly wrong when what's actually happening is avoidance rather than genuine unavailability. The difference between those two isn't obvious in the moment, which is precisely why it's worth building a real, honest test for which one is actually happening before calling it a day early again.
