Sixteen fills. One stock. Three and a half hours. That's the shape of today before we even get to the tickers — and it's the real headline, because the system was right, the stock was right, and I still finished negative. If you've read [the DKI post from last week](/posts/2026-08-10-dki-jwel/), you already know I flagged a fourth VWAP tap as a warning sign. ## Death by a thousand cuts: the WXM overtrade

<span class="pill pill-china">WXM · China</span> <span class="pill pill-hold">0.4M Float</span>

WXM is WF International Limited, a Chengdu-based electromechanical solutions company — HVAC systems, floor heating, water purification — serving large commercial developments across Sichuan. It's a Cayman Islands-registered, Nasdaq-listed name with a genuinely thin **0.4 million share float**, and it had already formed a strong impulse leg from **$4.28 to $11.70**, breaking both its 200-day moving average and a 10-month high before I ever opened a position.

According to my own tracked sample of 39 big movers, Industrials sits right behind Basic Materials, Consumer Defensive, and Communication Services for producing the largest average moves — [you can see that data here](https://highbreakers-wldeu97.public.builtwithrocket.new/).

### The setup was never the problem

VWAP was already providing a base for entry, reinforced by the 9-EMA on the 15-minute chart. The plan was sound: wait for accumulation, let impulsive traders get shaken out, let the stock continue its trajectory. What actually happened was sixteen fills of me not waiting.

### The setup that actually earned the entry

At **8:50 AM**, price tapped VWAP for a **fourth time** — but this time, at proper intervals, not in tight succession. Two 15-minute indecision candles formed right at the confluence of the 9-EMA on the 30-minute chart, the VWAP, and the 200-day EMA. Three separate technical references agreeing on one price, spaced out over real time instead of chopped through in a few minutes.

{{< callout type="important" title="Tap count was never the actual rule. Spacing was." >}}
Four sessions ago on DKI, a fourth VWAP tap failed, and I wrote that rule down as "3+ tight taps means the level is exhausted." That rule survives today, but it needed a correction I hadn't made explicit yet: **the danger was never the number four. It was the word "tight."** WXM's fourth tap, spaced properly over hours with real indecision candles and triple-confluence backing it, was one of the cleanest setups of the week. Same count, opposite quality, because the underlying mechanic — a level being tested by real two-sided participation over time, versus a level being chewed through in a few rushed minutes — was completely different.
{{< /callout >}}

The correctly-identified setup produced a **$41 gain**, even at half the intended size, and briefly pulled the day back into positive territory. Then, after the open, I took a couple more trades near **$11.84** based on a rule I do genuinely hold — that a strongly moving stock which sells off hard and bases out has good odds of making a higher move. It's a real rule. It just failed this time; a panic-buy setup that didn't confirm. That swing cost $23 and closed the day at -$16.

WXM itself kept moving without me — a real buying opportunity opened around **$9.13** and ran all the way to **$14** before an unexplained panic sell-off. I didn't catch the reason, because I wasn't watching Level 2 by that point, and I hadn't recorded the session on video to review afterward either. Both of those are fixable process gaps, not trading mistakes exactly, but they're the reason I can't fully explain today's most important move.

## PLAG: the trade I dismissed for the wrong reason

<span class="pill pill-hold">11.64M Float</span>

While WXM ate my morning, PLAG quietly handed out a real one. Planet Green Holdings Corp. is, on paper, a diversified consumer-products and online-advertising holding company — but the move today was pure catalyst: Planet Green announced it had entered commercial operations to source, market, and distribute **lactoferrin**, a glycoprotein used in infant formula, immune supplements, and sports nutrition, sometimes nicknamed "Pink Gold." China alone represents an estimated 22% of a $300 million global lactoferrin market, growing roughly 9% a year — and the news was enough to send the stock up as much as **792.7%** on the day, according to real-time financial press. My own notes had it running from a base of **$0.579** to a high of **$1.68**, meaning the real move likely ran considerably further past where I was watching.

I dismissed the stock because it traded under $3. That's it. That's the whole reason.

{{< callout type="tip" title="What 'at least $500' actually means" >}}
By my own math, 100 shares from around $1, ridden even partway toward where PLAG actually traded that day, clears $500 without much difficulty — and given the reported 792.7% figure, that estimate may be conservative. I'd also have had real pyramiding room: PLAG showed none of the shake-off behavior that makes adding to a position dangerous, which means size could have grown as conviction did.
{{< /callout >}}

I'll be honest about something else: I don't fully understand what a lactoferrin market-entry announcement means for a diversified holding company's fundamentals, and I don't especially care to. What I care about is that the market reacted violently to the news, and the chart told the same story it always tells when that happens — impulse leg, retracement, crawl along the 9-EMA, opportunity. Understanding the catalyst is nice. Reading the reaction is the actual job.

## What today actually proves

I do not fail at spotting big movers. WXM and PLAG were both sitting in plain view, both matched patterns I already trade, both got flagged before the move happened. What I fail at is mechanical execution — sticking to a rule once it's already been correctly identified, instead of re-litigating it in real time under the pressure of a live chart. You don't need advanced math to trade well. You need arithmetic, percentages, and the discipline to do the boring thing you already decided to do three hours ago. That third part is the entire game, and it's the only part that isn't a knowledge problem.
