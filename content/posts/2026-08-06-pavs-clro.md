I opened my charts at seven o'clock this morning and found a session already in motion without me. CLRO, WYHG, and PAVS had all made their moves before I'd poured coffee. I don't trade the pre-market as a rule — the moves there tend to be thin and overextended before I can trust them — and that rule is still a good one. What wasn't good was forgetting that yesterday's session had been genuinely wild, and wild sessions have a way of throwing off sympathy moves the next morning. I should have been at my desk earlier watching for exactly that. I wasn't.

## PAVS: a $33 million deal closing in real time

<span class="pill pill-emerald">Consumer Products · Digital Commerce</span> <span class="pill pill-hold">857.1K Float</span>

PAVS is Paranovus Entertainment Technology Ltd., a Cayman Islands-incorporated, Nasdaq-listed company that's spent the last year quietly reinventing itself — away from its legacy e-commerce and auto-sales lines and toward AI-powered games, TikTok-linked digital commerce, and now, evidently, activewear. Exchange data still tags it as a Consumer Defensive packaged-foods name, which is one of those small, funny reminders that sector classifications on micro-cap shells lag the actual business by months or years — nobody updates the label the moment a company pivots.

What actually moved the stock this morning was concrete: PAVS closed its acquisition of the athletic wear brand **Heyviva** from Jabanero Inc., an asset purchase carrying a **$33 million** price tag paid out in staged cash installments. The deal picked up U.S. trademarks, customer data, inventory, and social accounts, while EU and UK trademark rights stayed with Jabanero under a right-of-first-refusal. It closed as an asset deal rather than the equity acquisition originally floated back in June — a detail that matters, because asset deals close faster and cleaner than equity ones, which is part of why the market reacted the moment the filing hit rather than waiting for a longer integration timeline.

### What the chart actually did

Price reacted cleanly off the **9-EMA on the 30-minute chart**, then spent time crawling along the **12-EMA on the 15-minute chart** — the kind of orderly, average-respecting move that's genuinely easy to trust once you've seen it enough times. From a base of **$4.96**, the stock ran **143.22%** to a high of **$12.06**.

### Where it stands as I write this

At **7:45 AM**, PAVS is trading at **$9.70** — a level that's acting as strong overhead resistance right now. If it holds here through the rest of the pre-market and into the open, there's a real case for a strong breakout continuation once regular volume shows up. I'm not in this trade yet. I'm watching it.

## CLRO: a communications company that isn't one anymore

<span class="pill pill-hold">0.74M Float</span> <span class="pill pill-slate">Recent Mover Since July 2</span>

CLRO's story is a sharper pivot than PAVS's. ClearOne Inc. spent years as an audio and video conferencing equipment maker before divesting that entire legacy business to Biamp last October. What's left is essentially a public shell now merging with **Cortigent**, a neurostimulation and brain-computer-interface developer spun out of Vivani Medical. Once the deal closes, the combined company becomes Cortigent Holdings, trading under a new ticker, CRGT — Cortigent's technology centers on precision neurostimulation aimed at helping patients recover sight and motor function. The **8-K filing confirming the merger** is exactly the kind of catalyst that turns a sleepy micro-cap into a multi-week momentum story, and that's precisely what's been happening since the news broke on **July 2nd**.

### The chart's structure

Price held the **9-EMA on the hourly chart for a full hour** — real patience from the tape, not a quick fake-out. When it broke through the **200-EMA weekly level at $8.10**, it did so with two strong green candles, a pullback into two smaller red candles, and then a base built directly on that same 200-EMA weekly level before launching to **$13**. The daily chart's resistance at **$12.21** is worth flagging explicitly — the move pushed through it, which on its own is a meaningful technical event.

Different catalysts, different sectors entirely, but the same underlying mechanic: a thin float, a real corporate event, and a market that reacts to both at once.

## The one I only glanced at: WYHG

Wing Yip Food Holdings Group, a mainland-China meat product processor listed on Nasdaq, was the third name moving before I opened my charts. I didn't dig into its specific levels this morning, but it's worth naming for a reason bigger than today: WYHG, alongside yesterday's YXT and INLF, forms a pattern I keep seeing and keep underreacting to — thin-float, China-domiciled Nasdaq names that tend to move in loose sympathy with each other once one of them breaks out. Yesterday it was YXT and INLF. This morning it was CLRO, WYHG, and PAVS moving inside the same hour. That's not a coincidence I should keep treating as one.

## What today is actually about

My rule against trading the pre-market is sound, and I'm not walking it back — the moves there really are thinner and easier to misread than they look. But there's a difference between *not trading* the pre-market and *not watching* it. The setups on PAVS this morning were exactly the kind I've traded well before: clean EMA reactions, honest VWAP rejections, nothing exotic. I didn't miss them because they were hard to read. I missed them because I wasn't at the desk to read them.

Yesterday's session was loud enough that a follow-through morning like this one was close to predictable. Tomorrow, that's the actual adjustment — not a new setup to learn, just showing up early enough to watch the ones I already know how to trade.
