## The catalyst: Why was NXL moving?

Before examining the technical setup, it is worth understanding what brought NXL into the spotlight.

Nexalin Technology is a medical-device company developing non-invasive neurostimulation technology. Its Deep Intracranial Frequency Stimulation (DIFS™) platform is intended for applications involving mental health and neurological conditions.

Two developments were particularly relevant around October 1.

### 1. The South American commercialization agreement

On October 1, Nexalin announced a definitive exclusive distribution and local manufacturing agreement covering Brazil and South America.

The agreement concerns its ANVISA-approved Nexalin Sync™ device and establishes an initial 100-device commercialization program across seven countries.

However, there is an important distinction between the headline program and the actual binding commitment.

The initial binding purchase order covers only 10 devices. The broader 100-device program should therefore not be interpreted as 100 devices already ordered or purchased.

Why does this matter?

Because the market can react to the potential commercial opportunity long before the company demonstrates substantial recurring revenue.

For a low-float stock, even a relatively modest commercial announcement can attract disproportionate speculative attention.

### 2. Nasdaq compliance restored

On October 1, the company also disclosed that Nasdaq had formally notified it that it had regained compliance with the minimum \$1.00 bid-price requirement.

This followed a difficult listing-compliance period.

However, the company still had a separate condition to satisfy: demonstrating at least \$2.5 million in stockholders' equity by January 4, 2027.

Consequently, regaining bid-price compliance did not eliminate every listing-related risk.

**My interpretation:** The combination of a commercialization announcement and the removal of the immediate minimum-bid-price deficiency provided two distinct reasons for renewed attention. Neither guarantees that the company's commercial prospects or financial position have fundamentally improved.

Sources: Nexalin's October 1 SEC filings and company announcement.

### The first significant retracement

After the initial surge, NXL retraced approximately 20.73%, accompanied by another 570,000 shares in trading volume.

This was not the kind of instantaneous panic liquidation that I particularly enjoy trading.

Instead, the decline developed over fourteen consecutive one-minute candles.

That distinction matters.

A rapid liquidation can sometimes create an opportunity when sellers exhaust themselves and bids begin absorbing the selling pressure.

A prolonged decline, on the other hand, requires more patience. It gives the trader an opportunity to examine whether the stock is finding genuine support or simply continuing to distribute shares.

In this case, the decline brought price directly to an interesting technical confluence.

**A profitable exit is not automatically a good exit.**

A good exit should be consistent with the trade's original thesis, risk parameters, and management plan.

## Trading halts: The opportunity and the danger

The first halt lasted approximately ten minutes.

When trading resumed, the stock surged strongly, showing little hesitation before entering another halt within approximately fifteen seconds.

I made my second significant mistake of the day.

I closed the entire position.

The second halt lasted another ten minutes, after which the stock eventually advanced toward \$11 before making a strong decline.

The Level 2 order book began displaying substantial sell-side liquidity.

The ask side was flooding with large displayed share sizes, and I interpreted the activity as a warning that the momentum was deteriorating.

I was satisfied with the profit and did not attempt to participate in the subsequent decline.

### A note about Level 2

Large displayed ask sizes can be useful information, particularly around important price levels.

However, displayed liquidity is not the same as executed selling.

Orders can be modified, cancelled, replenished, or absorbed.

Therefore, a large ask should be evaluated alongside:

- Actual executed volume.
- Whether buyers are lifting the ask.
- Whether the displayed liquidity is replenished.
- How price responds to the selling pressure.
- Whether bids continue to support the market.

The order book is useful because it shows intentions and available liquidity at a particular moment. It is not a guarantee of what will happen next.

### The two exits I need to study

| Decision | My action | What followed | Lesson |
|----------------|----------------|----------------|------------------------|
| First trade | Closed for approximately \$7 | Price continued higher | My exit was too early relative to the original setup |
| Second trade | Closed 50 shares before the second halt | Price subsequently advanced toward \$11 | I protected the profit but potentially sacrificed additional upside |
| Final decision | Closed the laptop | No further trades | Protected the day's result from unnecessary exposure |

I do not want to replace premature profit-taking with reckless holding.

The objective is not to hold every position until the stock reverses.

The objective is to develop a repeatable exit methodology that allows winners enough room while protecting capital when the original setup fails.

## The account recovery: A small number with a bigger meaning

I have placed well over a thousand trades at this point.

I have also blown three trading accounts, losing approximately \$10,000 over the past five years.

Despite the experience, I have not yet achieved consistent profitability.

That is difficult to admit.

But I think the explanation may be simpler than I have made it.

I may have spent too much time searching for better setups while neglecting the most important variable: position size.

I have often traded with sizes that I was not psychologically comfortable managing.

Even a 100-share position can become dangerous when the stock is moving violently and the trader is not prepared to accept the possible loss.

The problem is not necessarily the number of shares.

It is the relationship between position size, volatility, stop distance, and account equity.

My losses have frequently taken the account down by more than 3%.

That is no longer acceptable.

From now on, I want to impose a maximum planned loss of 2% of account equity per trade.

The 2% limit is a ceiling, not a target.

There is no reason to risk the entire \$35 simply because the rule permits it.

For a volatile low-float stock, the actual risk should be determined by the technical invalidation level and the distance between entry and stop.

### Position sizing formula

**Position size = Maximum dollar risk ÷ (Entry price − Stop price)**

For example, suppose I enter at \$6.30 and the setup becomes invalid below \$6.10.

The difference is \$0.20 per share.

At \$35 of planned risk:

<div class="formula">
  <strong>Shares = 175</strong>
  <br>
  <span>35 ÷ (6.30 − 6.10) = 175</span>
</div>

This gives a theoretical position of 175 shares, before accounting for slippage, commissions, and execution uncertainty.

In practice, I would want to leave additional room for slippage, especially when trading stocks capable of entering volatility halts.

A stop order does not guarantee a maximum loss during a halt or a sudden liquidity vacuum.

## My trading criteria going forward

I do not want to trade simply because a stock is moving.

I want the market to present a recognizable setup at a meaningful level.

The following conditions will guide my entries.

### 1. Double or triple bottom at a strong psychological level

I want to see price test a significant level repeatedly and demonstrate an ability to hold it.

A double bottom alone is insufficient.

I want to observe how price reacts around the level, whether selling pressure diminishes, and whether buyers subsequently regain control.

### 2. Three bounces off VWAP

VWAP is an important reference for intraday trading.

When price repeatedly finds support around VWAP, it can indicate that buyers are willing to defend that area.

However, I need to distinguish genuine support from repeated weak tests that eventually result in a breakdown.

The quality of each bounce matters more than the number itself.

### 3. At least two bounces off the 9-EMA on higher timeframes

I will pay particular attention to:

- 30-minute
- 45-minute
- 1-hour
- 2-hour
- 3-hour
- 4-hour

I want to see price respecting the 9-EMA on a higher timeframe rather than relying exclusively on one-minute signals.

A lower-timeframe entry that aligns with higher-timeframe support offers a more structured trade thesis.

### 4. Level 2 activity at critical price levels

I want to observe the interaction between buyers and sellers around:

- Monthly highs and lows.
- Psychological price levels.
- VWAP.
- Significant moving averages.
- Previous intraday highs.
- Areas of heavy trading activity.

I will pay particular attention to actual executions and price response, rather than treating large displayed orders as guaranteed support or resistance.

### The new entry checklist

| Condition                               | Required? |
|-----------------------------------------|-----------|
| Clearly defined technical level         | Yes       |
| Recognizable price structure            | Yes       |
| Confirmation from price action          | Yes       |
| Level 2 observation at critical level   | Yes       |
| Predefined invalidation point           | Yes       |
| Position sized according to risk        | Yes       |
| Emotional readiness to accept the loss  | Yes       |
| Trade justified by a recognizable setup | Yes       |

If the setup is unclear, I will not force an entry.

If the trade has already moved substantially without me, I will not chase it simply because I am afraid of missing the opportunity.

## The dilution problem: What was happening behind NXL's price action?

There is another aspect of NXL that deserves attention.

The company has a significant history of share issuance, financing arrangements, and reverse stock splits.

For low-float traders, this is not merely a fundamental consideration.

It can directly affect how a stock behaves during a momentum event.

A stock can have an extremely small effective trading float while simultaneously having a substantial number of shares potentially available for future issuance.

These are two different things.

**Float is not the same as fully diluted share count.**

### Major dilution and capital-structure events

The following timeline is based on Nexalin's SEC disclosures available by October 1, 2026.

| Date | Corporate action | Details | Trading significance |
|----------------|----------------|-----------------------|------------------|
| April 2025 | ATM financing established | Equity distribution agreement initially authorized up to approximately \$9.78M; subsequently reduced and amended | Company had a mechanism to issue shares into the market |
| May 2025 | ATM reduction | Maximum offering amount reduced to approximately \$3.10M | Modified the financing capacity |
| October 2025 | ATM amended | Additional amendment to the equity distribution agreement | Continued financing flexibility |
| 2025 | ATM share sales | 691,407 shares sold during 2025 for approximately \$643,000 gross proceeds | Demonstrated actual share issuance |
| March 2026 | ATM sales disclosed | Additional 1,395,300 shares sold after year-end for approximately \$780,000 gross proceeds | Continued supply entering the market |
| August 19–20, 2026 | Registered direct offering | 2,419,354 shares at \$0.31, approximately \$750,000 gross proceeds | Direct dilution at a substantial discount to the subsequent reverse-split-adjusted market price |
| August 19–20, 2026 | Concurrent PIPE warrants | Warrants covering up to 1,209,677 shares, exercisable at \$0.50 before applicable adjustments | Potential additional supply |
| August 19, 2026 | Any Market Purchase Agreement | Financing arrangement allowing purchases of up to \$15M, subject to contractual and regulatory limitations | Potential future issuance |
| August 26–31, 2026 | 1-for-30 reverse split | Every 30 shares combined into one | Reduced share count mechanically; did not itself create economic value |
| August 19–20, 2026 | Additional registered offering | 80,645 reverse-split-adjusted shares at \$9.30 and warrants covering up to 40,323 shares at \$15.00 | Additional financing and potential warrant-related supply |
| September 3, 2026 | Resale registration statement | Up to 5,752,823 shares registered for resale, including AMPA-related shares, commitment shares and warrant shares | Important potential supply overhang, subject to applicable issuance and resale conditions |
| October 1, 2026 | Nasdaq bid compliance restored | Minimum bid-price deficiency resolved; separate equity requirement remains | Reduced immediate bid-price compliance uncertainty, but did not remove all listing risk |

**Important:** Registered resale shares are not necessarily newly issued shares on the registration date. Some represent shares already issued, while others are contingent on future issuance or warrant exercise. Registration itself does not mean that all registered shares have entered the market.

### The August financing deserves particular attention

The August 2026 registered direct offering involved:

- 2,419,354 common shares at \$0.31.
- Approximately \$750,000 in gross proceeds.
- Concurrent warrants covering up to 1,209,677 additional shares.
- A \$0.50 warrant exercise price.

These terms were disclosed in the company's August 20 SEC filings.

The offering was priced before the subsequent 1-for-30 reverse split.

On a purely mechanical post-split basis, the offering price corresponds to \$9.30 per share and the warrant exercise price corresponds to \$15.00.

This is an important distinction when comparing financing prices against current chart levels.

### The \$15 million financing arrangement

The August 2026 Any Market Purchase Agreement (AMPA) gave the company the right, but not the obligation, to sell up to \$15 million of shares to Alumni Capital.

The agreement specifies purchase-price mechanisms linked to recent trading prices, with contractual discounts and per-notice limitations.

The September S-1 disclosed that up to 5,600,000 AMPA shares were included in the registration statement, alongside commitment shares and warrant-related shares.

However, the filing also describes Nasdaq-related limitations that can constrain issuance.

Therefore, the entire registered amount should not be treated as immediately available unrestricted float.

For a trader, the practical question is:

**How many additional shares can realistically become available, at what prices, and under what conditions?**

That is more useful than simply looking at the headline registration amount.

### The reverse split

Nexalin implemented a 1-for-30 reverse split effective August 28, 2026, with split-adjusted trading beginning August 31.

A reverse split does not create value by itself.

It reduces the number of outstanding shares and proportionately adjusts the per-share price.

For traders studying historical dilution, every pre-split offering price, warrant strike, and share count must be adjusted before comparison with post-split chart prices.

This is particularly important when calculating potential dilution against a small float.

## What the dilution means for a low-float trader

I do not need to know the exact number of shares that will eventually enter the market to understand why the capital structure matters.

The existence of potential supply changes how I should approach the trade.

| Observation | Possible implication |
|-----------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Small trading float | Relatively modest buying pressure can produce large price movements |
| Large registered resale pool | Potential additional selling supply |
| Pre-funded or common warrants | Future share issuance may occur if contractual conditions are satisfied |
| ATM or equity-purchase facility | Company may have flexibility to raise capital through share sales |
| Reverse split history | Historical price and share-count comparisons require adjustment |
| Commercialization announcement | Can attract momentum independently of near-term financial performance |
| Nasdaq compliance uncertainty | Can add a separate source of volatility |

The most important lesson is that low float does not necessarily mean limited future supply.

The two characteristics can coexist.

And when they do, a trader should be especially careful about holding a position simply because the stock has been moving upward.

I was not trying to recover every previous loss in a single session.

- Recognized a strong technical confluence at \$6.23.
- Identified the importance of the 50-EMA, daily moving average, and VWAP.

- Observed changing Level 2 activity near the highs.

- Avoid exiting profitable positions without a structural reason.
- Develop a more objective approach to holding through halts.
- Define partial profit-taking levels before entering.

- Stop trading when my predetermined daily conditions are met.

There is a difference between being disciplined and being excessively defensive.

I want to become better at distinguishing between the two.

## The real lesson: Keep the winners going and cut the losers quickly

This is the lesson I am taking away from October 1.

I have spent years trying to develop the necessary skills and strategies to succeed in day trading.

I have studied price action, liquidity, technical levels, volume, and the behavior of low-float stocks.

But none of that matters if I repeatedly expose my account to losses that are disproportionately large.

A trader does not necessarily need a more complicated strategy.

Sometimes the missing component is simply the ability to execute an existing strategy without allowing greed, fear, and account pressure to interfere.

My three blown accounts are evidence that understanding the market and managing risk are two different skills.

I have to continue developing both.

Today's \$89.30 is not proof that I have finally solved the problem.

It is one positive session.

But it is a session in which I remained calm, recognized a setup, managed the risk, and walked away with a profit.

That is something worth documenting.

And tomorrow, I will have to do it all over again.
